Features

Everything you need. Nothing you don’t.

VEDIT keeps the layout professionals know — media, preview, inspector, timeline — and strips away everything else. What remains is fast, native and keyboard-driven.

Multi-track timeline

Unlimited video and audio tracks. Drag clips to move, drag their edges to trim, split at the playhead and let magnetic snapping keep every cut tight.

Linked A/V clips

Drop a video that carries sound and VEDIT creates a linked audio twin on the audio track. Moving, trimming, splitting and deleting stay in sync — both halves, always.

Live composited preview

Every visible layer is composited frame-accurately, with a Full / Half / Quarter quality selector. 4K-safe: the preview is capped at 1920 px — exports never are.

Waveforms & thumbnails

Audio clips draw their real waveform and video clips draw a thumbnail filmstrip, right on the clip. You see the edit, not just colored rectangles.

Transitions & fades

Overlap two clips for a cross-dissolve, or add a fade in and fade out to any clip — audio included.

Keyframed opacity & volume

Animate opacity and volume with keyframes and bend the values between them. Slow push-ins and music ducking without leaving the timeline.

Text & title clips

Add text and title clips for lower thirds, titles and cards — styled from the inspector and layered like any other video track.

Audio mixer

Per-track volume, pan, EQ and solo, plus a master volume fader. Mute and lock tracks directly from the timeline header column.

Color grading with .cube LUTs

Load industry-standard .cube LUTs to grade your footage — the same files you already use in other editors, applied per clip.

Hardware-accelerated encoding

Export on the GPU: NVENC (NVIDIA), Quick Sync (Intel) and AMF (AMD) encoders, selectable right in the export dialog.

Every format, every codec

Import any video, audio or image your ffmpeg can demux. Export H.264, H.265, VP9, AV1, ProRes, FFV1 or GIF — controlled by bitrate or CRF.

Non-destructive by design

A full undo/redo history takes back every trim and move, and a project is one portable .vedit.json file: assets, timeline and settings together.

The timeline

Where the edit happens

An unlimited stack of video and audio tracks under a zoomable ruler — driven by the keyboard as much as by the mouse. Video tracks composite over each other; audio tracks mix together.

V2 / V1

Tracks

Stack video layers upward — higher tracks composite over lower ones — and mix as many audio tracks as the edit needs. Every track header carries mute, lock and delete controls, and holds an unlimited number of clips.

S

Clips

Drag to move — across tracks, too. Drag an edge to trim. Press S to split at the playhead. Snapping is magnetic, so clips lock to each other and to the playhead, and the ruler zooms from seconds down to frames.

A1 / A2

Linked A/V

Video with sound arrives as a linked pair: the picture lands on a video track, its audio twin on the first free audio track. They move, trim, split and delete together, so picture and sound never drift out of sync.

Scrub by dragging the ruler or an empty lane, press Space to play, and step frame by frame with the arrow keys. The full map is in the shortcut table.

Build from source

Any OS with a stable Rust toolchain and ffmpeg + ffprobe on PATH:

shell 
git clone https://github.com/vedit-app/vedit.git
cd vedit
cargo run --release -p vedit-gui

Windows builds additionally need the MSVC C++ Build Tools; Linux needs libxkbcommon, libwayland and libx11 dev packages. See the README for the full details.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is VEDIT really free?

Yes. VEDIT is MIT-licensed open source: free for personal and commercial use, with no accounts, no telemetry and no watermarks. The complete source lives on GitHub.

Which platforms does it run on?

Windows is the primary target — every release ships an installer (VEDIT-Setup.exe) and a portable zip, built automatically by GitHub Actions. VEDIT also builds from source on Linux and macOS with cargo. Live audio in the preview is enabled on Windows and macOS; Linux previews play video-only, while exports always include audio on every platform.

Does VEDIT come with ffmpeg?

The ffmpeg binaries are not committed to the repository, but you never have to hunt for them: the Windows build script and the release workflow download official builds and place them next to vedit.exe. At runtime VEDIT finds ffmpeg automatically — next to the executable, in an ffmpeg/ folder, via VEDIT_FFMPEG_DIR, or on your PATH.

What can I import and export?

Anything ffmpeg demuxes: MP4, MKV, MOV, AVI, WebM, FLV and more as video; MP3, WAV, AAC, FLAC, OGG and more as audio; PNG, JPG, GIF, WebP, TIFF and more as images. Exports cover MP4, MKV, MOV, WebM, AVI, GIF, WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG and M4A containers with H.264, H.265, VP9, AV1, ProRes, FFV1, AAC, MP3, Opus and more — controlled by bitrate or CRF.

What exactly is a .vedit.json project?

A VEDIT project is a single JSON file that stores your media references, the full timeline and all settings. It is portable and human-readable — copy it to another machine, keep it in version control, or inspect it with any text editor.

How do the hardware encoders work?

In the export dialog, pick NVENC (NVIDIA GPUs), Quick Sync (Intel) or AMF (AMD) to render on your GPU. If your ffmpeg build lacks a hardware encoder, the software encoders — x264, x265, VP9, AV1 — are always available as a fallback.

Why so minimalist?

VEDIT keeps the Premiere-style layout — media, preview, inspector, timeline — and strips everything else. No nesting, no workspaces, no panels you never open. It starts instantly, renders fully native with egui, and leaves the heavy media lifting to ffmpeg.
